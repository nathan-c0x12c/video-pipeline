## 3. Why it worked

**Why they stopped scrolling:** A face already mid-sentence at 0.13s with a finger in the lens, plus a promise that costs nothing to hear out — *someone else* is about to show you something. There is no product to evaluate and nothing to disagree with in the first 3 seconds, so the only decision available is "hear the rest or not." The third-party framing is doing the heavy lifting: nobody is being sold to yet.

**What question kept them watching:** *"What's the app?"* — opened at 0.13, not answered until 8.17. Eight seconds is a long hold for a thin question, and the video nearly loses it in the 3.56–5.93 dead patch. What rescues it is that the answer, when it lands, is one sentence: *"TikTok meets DoorDash."* Two products already on the viewer's phone.

**Why they commented:** The concept is describable in four words, so everyone can have a take without watching twice. And the video ends with **no ask** — no name on screen, no link, no CTA — which leaves the comment box as the only available action. People filled the vacuum with the two things you comment when you like an idea and can't act on it: *protect this* (patent/trademark/Shark Tank) and *what's it called* (saying the name back). A CTA would have converted a chunk of that comment volume into installs.

**Who it excluded:** Anyone watching muted — which on TikTok is a large share. With sound off this video is two people on a sidewalk holding a phone; no name, no price, no claim survives. Also excluded: anyone wanting to evaluate the product, since the screen text is mirrored and unreadable, and the demo cuts out at 19–21s exactly where the second feature is introduced.

> **Thesis:** This worked because a sibling vouched for the product in the first four words and the whole thing was one unedited take, for a US urban delivery audience who recognise "TikTok meets DoorDash" instantly, and the proof is that the top comment cluster is *"get a patent"* — 615K likes on people trying to protect an idea, which is what happens when the concept lands and the video gives them nothing else to do.

Not a variant, a fix in all three: **stop mirroring the footage.** In the original, every price on screen reads backwards.

## 5. Can we shoot it

⚠️ **I don't have anything on Dietfit** — this section is written assuming it's a diet/nutrition tracking app. Correct me and I'll redo it; everything above is product-independent.

**Assets needed:**
- Two on-camera people with a **real** relationship (siblings, roommates, gym partners). The dynamic is the format. Two hired actors reading this will not survive contact.
- One phone with a build carrying **real, populated data** — real meals, real numbers, a real profile with real entries. Empty states kill it. This is the highest-effort asset and the one most likely to be faked and noticed.
- A street location with texture: scaffolding, storefronts, foot traffic. No studio, no clean wall.
- Nothing else. No lighting, no mic, no edit, no music licence. The original's production budget is zero and that is part of why it reads as real.

**Culture risk detail:** The original is a Black American sibling pair in Manhattan, and the delivery is specifically AAVE-inflected (*"y'all are gonna like it"*, the pointing, the volume that made a commenter say *"you ain't have to yell"*). **Do not cast around that performance.** A non-US team reproducing that cadence reads as imitation within two seconds and the comment section will say so. Shoot it in the register your actual cast speaks in — the transferable mechanic is *someone vouching for someone else*, not the accent. Second-order details that give away a non-US shoot: kilometres instead of miles, a non-US phone status bar, currency symbols, a delivery address format that isn't a US street address, and food choices that don't match the city you're claiming.

**Dietfit feature to show:** The one that has a **social or comparative** surface, on the assumption there is one — the original's strongest 4 seconds are the friends'-profile screen (23–27s), not the food feed. If Dietfit has a friends/leaderboard/shared-log view, that's the beat 2 payoff. If it doesn't, the *"X meets Y"* line has to carry the whole video, and the demo becomes a single feature shown well rather than two shown adequately. Tell me which and I'll write the beat sheet.

**Blocker:** **The one-line comparison.** The original's entire hinge is *"TikTok meets DoorDash"* — two products the viewer already has, one sentence, no explanation. Until Dietfit has a line that good, this format has nothing to hold at 8s and the video collapses into a feature walkthrough. That line is the pre-production deliverable; the shoot is easy by comparison.

Secondary blocker: the populated-data build. A demo of an app with three sample meals in it reads as a mockup, and the original's credibility comes from `LumLum · 2.6 miles away · $25.00` being obviously real.
