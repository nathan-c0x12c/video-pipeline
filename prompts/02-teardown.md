# Stage 2 — Teardown

Read `<OUT_DIR>/observation.md` (and the frames again where you need to look
closer). Write `<OUT_DIR>/teardown.md`.

Now you interpret. But every claim cites its evidence.

## The one rule

**Every claim points at a timestamp.**

"The hook works because the payoff is withheld until 4s" is a claim with
evidence. "The hook is strong" is a vibe. If you cannot attach a `t=` to a
sentence, it is not earned — cut it or go find the evidence.

Where stage 1 marked a coverage gap or `[unclear]`, you inherit that
uncertainty. Do not resolve it by assuming. Say "cannot tell from the sampled
frames" and move on. A teardown with three honest gaps is worth more than one
that is seamless and half-invented.

## Work through

**Hook — the first 3 seconds.** This is most of the outcome, so spend most of
the effort here.

- What is on screen at t=0? First spoken words? First on-screen text?
- What question, gap, or tension is opened — and when exactly is it closed?
- Cut count in the first 3s
- What would make someone swipe at t=1? Be specific and be harsh.

**Pacing.** Map cut rhythm from the cut list. Where does it slow? Does the
slow point coincide with something that earns the pause, or is it dead air?

**Retention risk.** Name the specific moments a viewer most likely leaves, with
timestamps and the reason. Rank them. Vague "the middle sags" is not a finding —
"t=8–11s holds one static shot with no new information and no text" is.

**Text and audio.** Does on-screen text carry the video without sound? Does it
duplicate the voiceover or add to it? Where do they fight for attention?

**Structure.** What shape is this — problem/solution, listicle, story, demo,
reveal? Where does the payoff land relative to the total duration?

**Close.** What is the last frame? Is there an ask, and is it earned by what
came before?

## Then the part that matters

**What to steal** — 3 to 5 techniques, each stated as a rule you could apply to
a different video tomorrow. Anchor each to the timestamp where it happens here.

Not "great pacing." Something like: "Open on the end state before explaining
how you got there — here the finished result is on screen at t=0 and the
process starts at t=2.5s."

**What not to copy** — what is working *despite* the execution, not because of
it. Follower count, an existing audience, a trending sound, luck. Being honest
here is what stops you cargo-culting a format that only worked for someone with
500k followers.

## Output

```markdown
# Teardown — <video id>

## Verdict
Three sentences. What this video does, and whether the hook earns the watch.

## Hook (0-3s)
Beat by beat with timestamps.

## Pacing map
Cut rhythm, slow points, whether each is earned.

## Retention risks
Ranked, each with a timestamp and a reason.

## Structure
The shape, and where the payoff lands.

## What to steal
3-5 portable rules, each anchored to a timestamp.

## What not to copy
What worked for reasons you cannot replicate.

## Confidence
What the frame sampling could not show. What stayed unclear.
```
